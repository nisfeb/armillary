## BTCPay facts, checked 2026-09-20 against the Greenfield swagger

- Auth header: `Authorization: token <api key>`.
- Create: `POST <url>/api/v1/stores/<store>/invoices` with JSON `{"amount": "<decimal dollars>", "currency": "USD", "metadata": {"ship": "<@p>", "nonce": "<nonce>"}, "checkout": {"redirectURL": "<url>", "redirectAutomatically": true, "expirationMinutes": 60}}`. The answer carries `id`, `checkoutLink`, `status`, `amount`, `currency`, `metadata`, `createdTime`, `expirationTime`.
- Read: `GET <url>/api/v1/stores/<store>/invoices/<id>`. `status` is one of `New`, `Processing`, `Expired`, `Invalid`, `Settled`. `additionalStatus` may say `PaidOver`, `PaidPartial`, `PaidLate`, `Marked`.
- Webhook: the store's webhook POSTs JSON with `type` (`InvoiceCreated`, `InvoiceReceivedPayment`, `InvoiceProcessing`, `InvoiceExpired`, `InvoiceSettled`, `InvoiceInvalid`, `InvoicePaymentSettled`), `invoiceId`, `storeId`, `timestamp`, `metadata`, and a `BTCPay-Sig` header equal to `sha256=` followed by the lowercase hex HMAC-SHA256 of the raw body with the webhook secret. Failed deliveries are retried up to six times.
- On chain, `Processing` arrives when the transaction is seen and `Settled` after the store's confirmation count (a block or more later). Over Lightning both arrive at once.

## 1. The pure lib

`code/lib/armillary-btcpay.hoon`, import-free, its own copies of the JSON getters and the HMAC the way `armillary-stripe.hoon` carries them (same header comment saying why). `+dollars-of |=(micro=@ud @t)`: microdollars to a decimal string with two places, rounded up to the cent (`1.000.000` is `1.00`, `1.505.000` is `1.51`, `5.000` is `0.01`). `+micro-of |=(t=@t (unit @ud))`: a decimal dollars string to microdollars (`de-dec` at scale 6). `+invoice-request |=([url=@t store=@t key=@t ship=@t nonce=@t micro=@ud redirect=@t] request:http)`: the create call above with `amount` from `dollars-of`. `+invoice-get-request |=([url=@t store=@t key=@t id=@t] request:http)`. `+read-invoice |=(body=@t (unit [id=@t link=@t status=@t amount=@t ship=@t nonce=@t]))` (`checkoutLink`, `metadata.ship`, `metadata.nonce`; `link` may be `''` on a read). `+event-of |=(body=@t (unit [type=@t id=@t]))` (`type`, `invoiceId`). `+verify-sig |=([secret=@t header=@t body=@t] ?)`: the header must start `sha256=`; compare the rest with the HMAC hex through a constant-time fold. `+settled |=(status=@t ?)` is `=('Settled' status)`. Tests in `tests/lib/armillary-btcpay.hoon`: `dollars-of` on the four cases above; `micro-of` on `12.34`, `0.5`, `x` (`~`); the create request's method, URL, `authorization` header value `token k`, and the decoded body's `amount`, `currency`, `metadata.ship`, `checkout.redirectURL`, `checkout.expirationMinutes`; the get request's URL; `read-invoice` on a `Settled` fixture with metadata and on a fixture with no metadata (ship `''`); `event-of` on an `InvoiceSettled` payload and on junk; `verify-sig` on a header computed in the test, on a wrong hex, and on a header without the prefix. About 14 arms.

## 2. Settings and the checkout

Settings gain `btcpay_url`, `btcpay_store`, `btcpay_key` and `btcpay_webhook_secret`; `mask-doc` already masks names ending `_key` and `_secret`; `+kept-secrets` keeps a blank incoming key or secret and clears it on `null`, exactly as for Stripe. A trailing slash on `btcpay_url` is stripped when read.

`+inbox-checkout`'s live branch for `rail` `btcpay`: the same refusals as Stripe's, written as `refused` rows with a note (`btcpay: not set` when url, store or key is blank; `plan: unknown`; `amount: below the minimum`; `plan and amount: choose one`; `plan: subscriptions are card only` for a subscription plan). A top-up plan's `price` is the amount. Then `+finish-checkout`'s BTCPay twin: `+fetch` the `invoice-request` with `redirect` `<public_url>/apps/armillary/pay/return?ship=<ship>&nonce=<nonce>&rail=btcpay`; on a 2xx `read-invoice`, `set-checkout` with `url` the `checkoutLink`, `sid` the invoice id, `status` `pending`, `expires` now plus one hour; on anything else `status` `refused`, `note` `btcpay answered <status>: <the body's message when JSON, else nothing>`. The `ponytail:` ceiling comment on the inbox fetch already exists; it covers both rails.

## 3. Verification

`+credit-btc-invoice |=(id=@t (fiber ,[ok=? why=@t]))`, both routes call it: `+fetch` the get request; `read-invoice`; `ship` must be an `@p` (`ship: not an @p`); the checkout row on that ship by `sid` (`unknown invoice` when none); when `status` is `Processing`, set the row `status` `processing` and answer ok `processing` (no credit); when `Expired` or `Invalid`, set the row to that word in lowercase and answer ok; when `Settled`, credit `micro-of amount` with `rail` `btcpay`, `ref` the invoice id, `note` `btcpay invoice` (deduped by the credit op, `already recorded` on a repeat), then `mark-paid`. `set-checkout` must accept a status update on an existing row without a new url: reuse the existing op with the row's stored fields, the way `mark-paid` does.

`POST /hooks/btcpay`, public: when `btcpay_webhook_secret` is set, `verify-sig` on the `btcpay-sig` header and the raw body; a bad or missing signature is 401 `signature` and a ring row `btcpay.webhook` not ok. `event-of`; `~` answers 200. `InvoiceSettled`, `InvoiceProcessing`, `InvoiceExpired`, `InvoiceInvalid`: `credit-btc-invoice` and a ring row `btcpay.webhook` with the outcome. Every other type 200 and nothing. Always 200 after a verified signature, the Stripe rule, since BTCPay retries.

`GET /pay/return` grows: with `rail=btcpay` and `nonce`, find the row by nonce on the `ship` in the query (this is the one place the query's ship is used, and only to locate the row; the invoice's own metadata is what credits), then `credit-btc-invoice` on its `sid`; `Settled` shows "Payment received"; `processing` shows "Payment seen, waiting for confirmations. Your balance updates on your ship once it settles, usually within an hour on chain and at once over Lightning."; anything else the pending text. The Stripe branch is unchanged.

## 4. The stub and the page

`scripts/fake-btcpay.py PORT SECRET SHIP_URL`: `http.server`, threaded, no dependencies, an in-memory store. Requires `Authorization: token <anything non-empty>` on the API routes (401 otherwise). `POST /api/v1/stores/<store>/invoices` answers `{"id": "inv_<n>", "checkoutLink": "http://127.0.0.1:PORT/stub/pay/inv_<n>", "status": "New", "amount", "currency", "metadata", "createdTime", "expirationTime"}`. `GET /api/v1/stores/<store>/invoices/<id>` answers the record. `GET /stub/pay/<id>` is a browser page with three buttons; `POST /stub/pay/<id>` marks `Settled` and POSTs `InvoiceSettled` to `SHIP_URL/apps/armillary/hooks/btcpay` with `BTCPay-Sig` made with SECRET (skipped when SECRET is `-`); `POST /stub/processing/<id>` marks `Processing` and POSTs `InvoiceProcessing`; `POST /stub/expire/<id>` marks `Expired` and POSTs `InvoiceExpired`. `GET /stub/state` dumps the store. Everything else 404.

The Payments view gains the BTCPay card: url, store id, key and webhook secret masked with `leave blank to keep`, the webhook URL to paste into the store as `<public_url>/apps/armillary/hooks/btcpay` with the four event types named, and the last ten `btcpay.*` ring rows beside Stripe's. The customer's Account view: the rail choice becomes real (`Card` or `Bitcoin`), subscription plans are card only and say so when Bitcoin is chosen, and a checkout row in `processing` shows "waiting for confirmations". The owner's account detail shows checkout rows with their rail and status.
